# Q&R Observations — published to date (as of Sept 5, 2026)

Source: quarterlineandrock.com/observations. Tagline on the page: *"Readings on capital in our region. Each reading answers one question about capital in our region, with a single measure."*

This is the house format. Every Lens candidate the system produces must look like one of these: a question, one measure stated as an everyday ratio, and a method note that names the source and states the bias honestly.

---

**006 · September 3, 2026 · Who owns the houses?**
Of every 100 single-family houses in St. Joseph, Elkhart, and Marshall counties, 82 are the owner's home, 14 belong to someone nearby, 2 to an owner elsewhere in Indiana or Michigan, and 2 out of state. For now, these houses mostly belong to the people who live here.
*Method.* County Auditor and Assessor parcel files, 2024 assessment, via Indiana DLGF Gateway; single-family parcels (class 510–515). Owner location is the mailing address, so the local share is a ceiling.

**005 · September 1, 2026 · How attractive are our homes to investors?**
The cities in our region screen at about an 8.4% gross rental yield, well above the roughly 5% national norm. The affordability that makes them a good place to live is the same thing that draws distant capital to buy the homes.
*Method.* Gross rental yield is annual median gross rent divided by median owner-occupied home value, from the Census American Community Survey, 2020–2024, for the region's cities, population-weighted.

**004 · August 27, 2026 · How much financing would it take to solve our succession problem?**
About $1.2 billion in business value changes hands in the region each year. Bank debt and seller notes cover most of it; roughly $290 million has to be buyer equity — the part in short supply.
*Method.* Each business valued at a typical EBITDA-based enterprise value by size and sector, financed by a capital stack scaled to the deal, with buyer equity as the residual; summed and spread over a 15-year window. Illustrative, not a forecast.

**003 · August 25, 2026 · How many small business buyers do we need for our succession problem?**
Roughly 320 businesses a year need a new owner across the five counties just to keep pace with retirements — on the order of hundreds of prepared, financed buyers, every year.
*Method.* Business counts (5–250 employees) from Census County Business Patterns; the 55-or-older ownership share from the Census Annual Business Survey; a 15-year transition window.

**002 · August 20, 2026 · Do our deposits matter to the big banks?**
To the national banks that hold a growing share of our deposits, this region is about 18¢ of every $100 — one market among hundreds, and a shrinking one. In 1994 about one of every $500 on deposit in U.S. banks was here; today, about one of every $1,300.
*Method.* FDIC Summary of Deposits, June each year, grouped by ultimate regulatory owner. The region is St. Joseph, Elkhart, and Marshall counties (IN) and Berrien and Cass (MI).

**001 · August 18, 2026 · How has ownership of our banks changed?**
At its peak 50 years ago, our region had 47 banks and thrifts headquartered here. Today, four remain. And 59¢ of every dollar on deposit now sits with an institution run from somewhere else.
*Method.* FDIC institution records and Summary of Deposits, June each year, for St. Joseph, Elkhart, and Marshall counties (IN) and Berrien and Cass (MI). Federal records begin in 1934.

## What these have in common (read this before building anything)

- **One question, one number.** Never a dashboard. Never three findings.
- **Everyday ratios.** 82 of 100 houses. 18¢ of every $100. One of every $1,300. Not 0.18%.
- **The method note states the bias.** "Mailing address, so the local share is a ceiling." "Illustrative, not a forecast." If a caveat would swallow the finding, the finding isn't published.
- **The stance.** Every one asks, in some form: where does control over this region's capital sit — here or elsewhere — and which way is it moving?
- **Sources so far:** Indiana DLGF Gateway parcel files; Census ACS, CBP, ABS; FDIC institution records and Summary of Deposits. All free, all public.
- **Only one (#005) compares the region to a national benchmark.** The others measure the region against itself over time (#001, #002), decompose it (#006), or construct an estimate from public inputs (#003, #004). Comparison is one move among several, not the default.
